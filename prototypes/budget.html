MTG Combo Finder

Prototype — what a suggestion costs. Combo counts are the tuning deck’s real ones, measured 13 Aug 2026. The prices are not real.

Placeholder prices. Scryfall is unreachable from the sandbox that drew this page, so every figure in a price position was typed by hand and is deliberately round — 0.25, 0.50, 1, 2, 4, 8 — so it cannot be mistaken for a quote. One card is deliberately unpriced. What is real: the cards, the combo counts, the seven-way tie, and the order each control puts them in.

The seven-way tie is the whole argument. Ashnod’s Altar, Heliod, Cleric Class, Light of Promise, Sunbond, Spider-Man and The Destined White Mage each unlock exactly 7 of this deck’s combos. Today the page orders those seven on EDHREC popularity, which is not the question anyone is asking of them. Switch Rank by and watch which one leads.

The default should be “combos, then cheapest”, and drawing the others is what settled it. Cheapest first puts Ajani’s Welcome (+5) above Cleric Class (+7) — it throws away the ranking the whole page exists to produce, in exchange for a number the reader could have read off the rows. Price belongs where popularity is now: after the count, deciding the 99% of the list the count leaves tied.

Combos per $1 is the flashy one and the least useful. It promotes a 25¢ card unlocking one combo over a $4 card unlocking seven, which is arithmetic rather than advice. It is on the control because it is cheap to offer and somebody will want it — not because it should be the default.

What a total may not claim. The basket says “about $14” and never a precise sum: these are market prices from a daily snapshot, one printing each, before postage. A page that prints $13.75 has told the reader something it cannot stand behind.
